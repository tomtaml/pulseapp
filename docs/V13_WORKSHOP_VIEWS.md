# V1.3 workshop views

The facilitator can open `https://pulse-srf-variant-preview.tom-tamlander.workers.dev/v13-setup.html`, choose a site and a starting view, then switch each question module on or off. The page creates a shareable preview link. No response is submitted or stored by these workshop views; record any approved observations using the workshop's separate procedure.

| Starting view | Scenario | Understanding questions | SUS | Service confidence, trust and outcome scales |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Demo only (`view=demo`) | Yes | No | No | No |
| Demo plus questions (`view=questions`) | Yes | Yes | No | No |
| Full preview (`view=full`) | Yes | Yes | Direct users only | Yes |

The facilitator can alter any of the last three columns with `questions=0|1`, `sus=0|1` and `scales=0|1`. The old `demo=1` link still forces demo only. A link with `view=` or an individual module switch always stays in preview mode, even if opened on an environment where the complete research instrument is available. Research submissions still require the complete approved instrument and server-side collection gates; these switches never relax the server validation.

The scenario pages appear in all views. The Finnish fleet route `/v13-fleet.html` runs the full RC1 mobile interaction stack: alignment guidance, next-delivery constraints, V2G permission and offer, animated charging and export cycle, override and winter recovery. Its questions, SUS and trust screens are enabled by the same facilitator switches. Links to `/v13.html?variant=fi-fleet` redirect to that route.

The Trikala route compares charging now, a lower tariff and renewable surplus with visible timing cards. Its energy screen shows grid-to-vehicle charging by default. A separate button can turn on an illustrative vehicle-to-grid flow and stop it again; choosing a charging window never grants permission.

The UK route has two clearly labelled scenes. Scene A shows guided street-bay wireless positioning, a simulated charging start and rain interruption with retry, conductive-gully and leave choices. Scene B is a separate illustrative overnight home setting: the participant parks the vehicle beside a house, chooses a protected-trip plan, then runs a paced six-checkpoint example from 22:00 to 07:00. The live card shows charge, energy direction, protected reserve and the current checkpoint. The participant can pause, resume, advance one checkpoint, skip to morning or replay. Only a separate `support_home` choice can show vehicle-to-home energy; a stop control cancels or stops sharing. The illustrative vehicle starts at 70%, its trip reserve is 65%, charging may reach 80%, and permitted home support may leave 75%. The card now shows a simulated energy ledger at every checkpoint: up to 6.0 kWh stored in the car, 3.0 kWh taken from it for V2H, 2.7 kWh delivered to the house, and a £0.36 illustrative energy cost difference. That calculation assumes 60 kWh usable battery capacity, 90% delivery, 30p/kWh household import and 15p/kWh replacement battery energy; recharge losses, wear and fees are excluded. These are workshop arithmetic, not measured energy or a live tariff, and canceling before export shows zero home delivery and zero cost difference. Household essential-load protection is a discussion prompt pending site values. The street bay is not portrayed as supplying the home, and the gully fallback does not imply V2H. Both routes use responsive cards and reduced-motion styles; the UK route retains large-text, high-contrast and read-aloud controls. All values and offers are illustrative, and the scenarios require site confirmation. These V1.3 pages do not change or deploy the partner V1.2 RC1 Worker.

SUS is presented only to roles marked as direct users: the fleet driver, Greek passenger driver and UK accessible driver. The dispatcher and fleet manager can see comprehension and confidence/trust items but not SUS. The Finnish fleet route uses the RC1 Finnish working translation for comprehension, SUS and trust when `lang=fi`; those are for cognitive testing and must not be treated as approved scores or the final V1.3 research instrument. Greek study questions and scales remain in English pending approval. UK assistive-technology and participant review is still needed before field use.
