# Oxfordshire V1.3 workshop instrument draft

Status: cognitive-testing preview, 28 September 2026. These Oxfordshire items are proposed workshop probes, not validated scales or approved field measures. The route stores no responses and cannot construct a V1.3 research submission. Record observations through the separately approved workshop procedure, including a spoken or printed route when the app is unsuitable.

## Participant flow

`view=demo` shows parking and overnight V2H only. `view=questions` adds the Oxfordshire task-experience page and four case-relevant understanding questions. `view=full` adds SUS for the direct user and the shared service-confidence, V2H-intention, accessible-control and actor-trust items. The `questions`, `sus` and `scales` URL switches continue to work independently. Draft task items may be left unanswered. The manual-arrow item appears only after that path was used.

| Draft item key | Participant-facing construct | Hypothesis / SRF mapping | Why ask after the task |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `uk_positioning_independence` | Positioning guidance and clear entrance route | UK-W2 adapted to home positioning; SRF-12, SRF-25 | Observe whether the participant could follow the illustrated positioning task independently. This does not test actual WPT alignment. |
| `uk_recovery_clarity` | Understanding what to check after a stop | UK-W3 adapted; SRF-08, SRF-27 | Compare the automatic obstacle stop and guidance-fault recovery. |
| `uk_manual_arrows` | Clarity of the forward → right → back arrow sequence | UK-W3 adapted; SRF-04, SRF-25, SRF-27 | Ask only if the participant used manual guidance; note incorrect arrows and help requested separately. |
| `uk_morning_reserve` | Control of morning mobility reserve | UK-H1; SRF-11, SRF-19 | Probe the selected 65–80% minimum and its effect on available household energy. |
| `uk_energy_direction` | Recognising charging versus V2H | UK-H2; SRF-12 | Check whether the direction can be identified without the facilitator explaining it. |
| `uk_household_value` | Understanding delivered household energy | UK-H1; SRF-03, SRF-12 | Distinguish energy taken from the battery from energy reaching the home. The cost comparison is illustrative. |
| `uk_override_control` | Finding the stop-sharing control | UK-H2; SRF-11, SRF-25 | Observe whether the participant actually tries Stop home support. |
| `uk_support_responsibility` | Knowing whom to contact after a service failure | UK-H3; SRF-10, SRF-12 | A low rating can expose the prototype's missing operator/support arrangement; ask the participant which actor they expected. |

The four understanding questions ask about obstacle and access-route review, the chosen morning minimum, V2H destination and who can stop sharing. The first option in each question is the intended answer for facilitator review; the app does not score these draft answers. The previous generic UK comprehension wording remains in the server contract solely for isolated synthetic tests and must be reconciled before any UK research collection.

The shared V1.3 confidence and trust items preserve cross-site comparison. The UK outcome page includes V2H intention and accessible-control experience, and omits street-WPT intention because no street scene is shown. SUS retains its existing ten-item form; it measures the interface experience, not physical accessibility or actual V2H acceptance. Do not combine the draft Oxfordshire items into a psychometric score.

## Facilitation checks outside the screen

- Ask the participant to show how they would stop, recheck the area, follow the arrow guidance and request assistance. Observe task completion, wrong-direction attempts and help needed rather than interpreting agreement ratings alone.
- Ask what mobility journey and essential household loads must be protected, who may change each rule, and who is responsible for an accessible fault response.
- Use a separate accessible storyboard for cable handling, street layout and other users of public space (UK-W1 and UK-S1). The home V2H scene cannot establish those outcomes.
- Test the controls with participants' own input and assistive-technology methods and offer a non-digital route. Do not infer accessibility from a keyboard or automated test alone.

References for the review approach: [W3C WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/wcag/) on keyboard focus and status messages; [GOV.UK research with users who do not use digital services](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/understanding-users-who-dont-use-digital-services) on direct research and assisted routes. These sources guide accessibility evaluation; they do not validate the draft items above.
